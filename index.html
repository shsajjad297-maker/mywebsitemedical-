📚 How to Read Medical Subjects

A practical guide for MBBS students about textbooks, resources and examination strategy.

🦴 Anatomy

How to Study Anatomy

Anatomy should be studied mainly from standard textbooks. General Anatomy basics should be understood early because they help in Gross Anatomy, clinical correlation and viva.

1. General Anatomy

  • Main Book: AK Datta (Standard textbook)
  • Vishram Singh can be used for better diagrams and figures.
  • Guide books are mainly useful before written examinations.

2. Gross Anatomy

  • Main Book: AK Datta (Except Neuro Anatomy)
  • Figures and Clinical Anatomy: Vishram Singh
  • For advanced clinical correlation: Snell's Clinical Anatomy and Moore's Anatomy can be consulted.
  • Guide books should be read before professional examinations.

3. Neuro Anatomy

  • Vishram Singh Neuro-Anatomy - Commonly followed textbook.
  • Snell's Neuro-Anatomy - Gold standard reference.

❤️ Physiology

Physiology is the foundation for future clinical subjects, especially Medicine.

Recommended Books

  • General Physiology → Guyton
  • Blood Physiology → Samson Wright + Guyton figures
  • CVS Physiology → CC Chatterjee
  • Endocrine → Guyton (Must read)
  • CNS → CC Chatterjee
  • Renal Physiology → Guyton + ABC Biochemistry

Study Strategy

  • If enough time → Read Guyton.
  • If time is limited → Use Guide + Lecture Slides.
  • Concept-based videos help before reading difficult topics.
  • Dr Najeeb and Shoumen Manna are useful learning resources.

🧬 Biochemistry

ABC Biochemistry is sufficient for undergraduate MBBS level.

Recommended Books

  • Main Book: ABC Biochemistry
  • Lecture slides should be studied along with ABC.
  • Extra information should be added beside relevant topics.
  • Metabolism, Food and Genetics can be further studied from Harper and Lippincott.

Study Strategy

  • ABC + Lecture slides cover most examination requirements.
  • Videos can help to clear difficult concepts.
  • Dr Shanmugapriya Madam's videos are useful for Metabolism and Genetics.
